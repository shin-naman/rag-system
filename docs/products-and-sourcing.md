# Products and Sourcing

## House Blends

Nimbus sells three house blends, each named after a cloud type to match the
company's theme. Stratus is a light roast with bright, tea-like acidity and
floral notes; it is the recommended starting point for people who normally find
coffee too bitter. Cumulus is a medium roast, balanced and slightly sweet, and it
is the most popular drink by volume — the Cumulus pour-over accounts for roughly a
third of all café orders. Thunderhead is a dark roast, bold and smoky, aimed at
customers who want a traditional strong cup.

All beans are sold as whole bean in 12-ounce bags and ground to order in-store.
Nimbus does not sell flavored coffee or pre-ground coffee. The founders have been
firm about this: they argue that pre-ground coffee goes stale far too quickly and
that flavored syrups mask the qualities of the bean. Customers who want their
beans ground can have it done at the counter, where staff will ask what brewing
method they use so the grind size is correct.

## Seasonal Offerings

In addition to the three house blends, Nimbus releases a rotating single-origin
coffee each season. These are produced in limited quantities and are usually sold
out within a few weeks. Seasonal offerings are announced through the email
newsletter and are not part of the standard menu.

## Sourcing

The company sources green coffee directly from farms in Ethiopia, Colombia, and
Guatemala. Nimbus signed its first direct-trade contract in 2018 with the Kiranga
cooperative in Ethiopia, and that relationship remains the source of the Stratus
light roast. Direct-trade contracts guarantee farmers a minimum price of $3.10 per
pound, which is above the Fair Trade baseline. Mara Delacroix visits partner farms
at least once a year to taste upcoming harvests and negotiate contracts in person.

Nimbus publishes an annual sourcing report that lists every farm it buys from, the
price paid per pound, and the volume purchased. The company says this transparency
is meant to hold itself accountable and to give customers a way to verify its
direct-trade claims.
